Barry O'Reilly and Cyrille Martraire explore how Residuality Theory can be combined with Domain-Driven Design (DDD) in this insightful talk. Residuality Theory, which places uncertainty and indeterminism at the center of software architecture, is brought to life through practical examples. The connection to DDD methodologies highlights ways to make systems more resilient to stressors and unpredictable changes.

The talk began with a theoretical introduction, which was at times challenging to follow, but it became increasingly relatable through practical and humorous examples. The collaboration between Martraire, an expert in DDD, and O'Reilly, the creator of Residuality Theory, offered exciting perspectives for architects and engineers.

### Key Points:

- **What is Residuality Theory?** Residuality Theory models systems based on their "residues" under stress. The goal is to achieve "Criticality," a balance that makes systems resilient to unpredictable changes.
- **Connection to DDD:** Residuality Theory can be used to validate DDD models, identify challenges, and test the boundaries of aggregates, subdomains, and bounded contexts.
- **Practical Application:** The theory uses stressors (fictitious scenarios) to test systems without considering probability or cost in early stages. This allows architects to iteratively improve their designs.
- **Post-Structuralism:** The approach challenges traditional, structural thinking in software architecture and focuses on flexibility and adaptability.
